Left — kernel layers on one vertical axis. A band is taller when more machinery actually hooks into it, so the silhouette already says where the complexity lives. The thin bar at the bottom of each band is that layer's current activity. Centre — two columns: TX descends, RX climbs. On the receive side a frame is drawn hollow until it crosses the sk_buff born line inside the driver band: before that point no socket buffer has been allocated yet, which is exactly why an XDP drop is the cheapest drop in the system. A red ring at NETFILTER is a drop. Right — mechanisms anchored at the height of the layer they attach to, split into path (the packet goes through it), hook (attached, and often simply not there) and table (the kernel looks into it, the packet does not flow through it). Grey dashed means detected-absent; blue dotted means the backend is not allowed to look — those two are deliberately not the same statement. Hover a chip for its source.
